## 正文

K-beauty just crossed an interesting threshold.

Korea's cosmetics exports hit $11.4B last year, enough to push Korea past the U.S. into #2 globally, behind only France. Exports to Europe were up 62.4% in H1 2026 alone.

But the more interesting shift isn't the export number. It's who's exporting the category now.

Brands like Yepoda (Berlin), hwarang' (Finland), and MiiN (Barcelona) aren't Korean companies selling into the West, they're Western companies adopting Korean formulation, manufacturing, and naming conventions to sell K-beauty to their own local markets. MiiN alone runs 55+ stores across five European countries and is growing revenue ~40% a year.

On the brand side, the capital is following. AXIS-Y - a vegan, climate-inspired skincare brand that built 90%+ of its revenue outside Korea before it even launched properly at home, just brought in MBK Partners for a 40% minority stake at a ~KRW 430B valuation. Founder keeps 60% and full management control. Twenty million+ units sold of one hero serum, no funding round required to get there.

And on Amazon US specifically, the category is showing real depth beyond the headline brands, some individual hero SKUs alone are estimated to be moving well into six figures in monthly revenue.

A few questions this raises for anyone building or investing in this space:

> Is "K-beauty" becoming a formulation/manufacturing standard rather than a country-of-origin label? 

> Does global-first, domestic-second (like AXIS-Y) become the new default playbook for the next wave of brands? 

> With PE now pricing K-beauty brands at scale, does US retail (Amazon, Target, Ulta) become the next major land-grab?
